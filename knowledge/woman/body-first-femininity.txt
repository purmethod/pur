PURE FOR WOMEN
PURE Method · for her · Paul's stance and the evidence behind it. Use this file only when the reader is a woman; for a man, ignore it.

═══ HOW TO USE THE METHOD FILES FOR HER ═══
The other method files were written for men. The method is the same for her: four pillars (P, U, R, E) and the roof above them. When you use those files for a woman:
- Keep every principle: start so low she barely notices it, one fixed wake time, the ladder, the pause between impulse and action, promises to herself, principles, the ego.
- Drop every line that only holds for men: testosterone, erections, ejaculation control, "as a man", "the man who", red flags in women, leading a woman. Never move a testosterone claim over to her.
- BODY FIRST (below) is the thread through her whole book and her P pillar.
- Relationship chapters (r3) are about her: her steadiness, her boundaries, what she lets in. Never tell her to submit, to obey, or to make herself smaller for a man.
- The ladder works for her too: wall or knee push-ups if needed, same 1-minute rests.

═══ PAUL'S STANCE (his view, not a study) ═══
- Society today tries to turn women into men and men into women. Paul wants women to shine in their feminine energy again: to be a woman, fully, without apology.
- A woman puts herself first. Not selfishly. Physically. She does not neglect her body: food, sleep, movement, breath, all of it. Her body is number one. That is the purpose.
- When she has found that, she does not become selfish. She can give more love, because she gives from fullness and not from emptiness.
- Femininity is not weakness and not a role she owes anyone. It is a strength she lives from the inside. She can work hard, earn, lead a team and still be soft at home and in her body. The point is that she no longer has to live in permanent fight mode.
- Write this as Paul's conviction, in his voice. Never present "feminine energy" as a scientific finding. It is a way of living, not a lab value.

═══ BODY FIRST (the thread through her book) ═══
— Core principle —
Most women look after everyone before themselves: partner, children, parents, work, friends. Their own body gets what is left: the late meal standing in the kitchen, the sleep that is cut short, the training that is cancelled first. Body first turns this around. Her body is not a project to fix or a machine that must function. It is her home, and it comes first, so that everything she gives afterwards comes from fullness.

— What it means in practice —
- Sleep is not negotiable. One fixed wake time, then a bedtime that protects it.
- She eats real meals, sitting down, enough. Not skipping and then grazing.
- She moves every day, and she builds strength, not only cardio.
- She keeps a few minutes every day only for her body (breath, stretching, a walk in daylight), and she keeps them when others want something from her.
- She feels her body instead of judging it: hunger, tiredness, her cycle, her energy.

— Start so low she barely notices it —
Day one is one thing only. "every day at 7:00 i get up, the same time, also on the weekend. that is all." or "i eat breakfast sitting down." Then it grows week by week.

— Science —
- Self-compassion and love: in a study of 104 couples, people who treated themselves with self-compassion were described by their partners as more affectionate, more intimate and more accepting, and their partners were more satisfied; self-compassion, not self-esteem, predicted the healthier relationship behaviour (Neff & Beretvas, Self and Identity, 2013). This is the evidence behind "she gives more love when she takes care of herself".
- Sleep and love: after a poor night, people reported more conflict with their partner the next day; in the lab, couples who had slept poorly felt more negative during a conflict talk, solved it worse and read their partner's feelings less accurately (Gordon & Chen, Social Psychological and Personality Science, 2014).
- Women are about 1.4 times as likely as men to suffer from insomnia (Zhang & Wing, Sleep, 2006). Her sleep deserves protection, not leftovers.

═══ HER BODY: WHAT IS DIFFERENT ═══
— Energy and food —
- Too little food over a long time is not discipline. Large, prolonged energy deficits harm hormones, bones and health; a missing period without pregnancy is a warning sign of too little energy, not a sign of success (IOC consensus on relative energy deficiency, Mountjoy et al., British Journal of Sports Medicine, 2023). She eats enough, especially when she trains.
- Iron: in US girls and young women aged 12 to 21, about 39 percent were iron deficient, most of them without anaemia (Weyand et al., JAMA, 2023). If she is tired, pale, cold or short of breath, she asks her doctor to check ferritin. She does not take iron without a test.
- Fasting: in women, intermittent fasting did not change estrogen or prolactin in the trials so far, and it lowered androgens in premenopausal women with obesity; the evidence is small (Cienfuegos et al., Nutrients, 2022). For her, an eating window is optional, never strict, never combined with too little food, and never during pregnancy, breastfeeding, while trying to conceive, with a missing period or with an eating disorder.

— Her cycle —
- Performance changes across the cycle are small: in a meta-analysis, performance in the early follicular phase (the days of the period) was at most trivially lower, the evidence was weak, and the authors advised a personal approach (McNulty et al., Sports Medicine, 2020). So no rigid "cycle syncing" rules. She notices how her energy moves through her month and adjusts intensity, without skipping her minimum.
- If she has no period, or a very irregular one, without pregnancy or menopause, a doctor checks it first.

— Strength —
- Strength training is for her too. In postmenopausal women with low bone mass, eight months of supervised heavy strength training twice a week raised bone density at spine and hip compared with light home exercise, with no serious injuries (Watson et al., LIFTMOR trial, Journal of Bone and Mineral Research, 2018). Strong muscles and bones are part of a long, free life.
- Pelvic floor: training the pelvic floor can cure or improve urinary incontinence in women (Dumoulin et al., Cochrane Review, 2018). It also helps her feel her body from the inside.

— Pregnancy and breastfeeding —
- Pregnant women without complications are advised to move about 150 minutes a week at a moderate pace where she can still talk (ACOG Committee Opinion 804, 2020). Only with her doctor or midwife.
- No breath holds, no hyperventilation, no ice baths, no fasting while pregnant or breastfeeding.

— Comparison —
- Ten minutes on Facebook left young women in a more negative mood than a neutral website, and women who tend to compare their looks noticed more flaws in face, hair and skin afterwards (Fardouly et al., Body Image, 2015). Comparison steals the feeling of being at home in her own body.

═══ HER FEMININE ENERGY: SIX FIELDS ═══
These are the fields the questionnaire reads for her. Paul's stance, applied:
1. Doing and being. Many women only feel worth something when they achieve. She learns that she is enough before she has done anything: an evening of doing nothing without guilt is training, not laziness.
2. Her body as home. Not a project, not an enemy. She feels it, she listens to it, she feeds it, she lets it rest.
3. Receiving. Help, a compliment, a gift, being led by someone she trusts. Receiving is not weakness; a woman who can only give burns out.
4. Soft and strong. She does not have to be hard to be taken seriously. Softness held from strength is power.
5. Intuition. She trusts her inner voice, even when she cannot explain it yet, and checks it with her head instead of overruling it.
6. Love from fullness. She gives love because she is full, not to be loved back. Fighting about who is right or who decides costs love on both sides.

— Tools (small, daily) —
- Ten minutes for her body every morning before anyone else gets her: daylight, breath, stretching or a short walk.
- One real meal a day eaten slowly, sitting down, without a screen.
- One evening a week with nothing planned.
- When someone offers help or a compliment: say "thank you" and nothing else.
- Before a decision: one breath, then ask her body what it feels, then her head.
- The phone stays out of the bedroom.
- Strength twice a week, pelvic floor every day, a few seconds at a time.
